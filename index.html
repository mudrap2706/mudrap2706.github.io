I am a Master's student in Information Systems at Northeastern University in Toronto, preparing for doctoral research on whether information systems actually serve the people who rely on them, and who they quietly leave behind.

My path runs through practice: user research for India's National Pension System with older users in mind, accessibility work on Canadian government websites, automation for a community non-profit, and IT work supporting a mixed workforce, including non-desk staff. Across all of it, I keep returning to the same gap: between a system that works on paper and one that works for the person in front of it.

Research Interests
—

When a system passes an accessibility check, what does it still miss about whether people can actually use it?

—

Who gets reliable access to public information and services online, and who is left to work around the gaps?

—

How should we evaluate AI and information systems after deployment: by who they reach and who they miss, not just by how they perform on average?

Research & Projects
Automation · Community Non-profit

Payment Verification Automation: Community Family Services of Ontario

Challenge — Registrations and payments lived on two disconnected platforms, so staff matched them by hand and client confirmations took two to three days.

Approach — A team project at Northeastern: a Python pipeline (Flask, IMAP, regex parsing) that reads payment emails automatically, plus a reconciliation dashboard where a person reviews the edge cases.

Outcomes

  • About 90% of payments now process automatically
  • Confirmations go out in real time instead of days later
  • Roughly 18 staff hours saved each week, at no added operating cost

Relevance — The cases automation can't handle, such as name mismatches and odd amounts, are exactly the people a system can miss. Designing for them is part of the work.

Data Management & Engineering

Toronto Bicycle Thefts: End-to-End Data Pipeline & BI Dashboard

GitHubFinal Report

Challenge — Ten years of open municipal crime data (39,000+ records) needed to move from raw CSV to a live, explorable analytics layer usable by non-technical stakeholders.

My role — Led data modeling and SQL analysis on a 3-person team: designed the MySQL schema, wrote 15 analytical queries spanning temporal, geographic, attribute, and multi-dimensional analysis, and architected 6 pre-aggregated views plus a master table optimized for the BI layer. Teammates handled cloud infrastructure/ETL and the QuickSight dashboard build.

Fig. 3. Full dashboard: KPI cockpit, geographic map, trend line, weekly risk matrix, and brand targeting, unified through a single master table for cross-filtering.

Outcomes

  • Schema and query design supporting sub-second dashboard performance across 39,000+ records
  • Recovery rates under 2% in every police division; 53.85% of thefts concentrated at outdoor/street and apartment locations
  • Master table architecture enabled global filtering without precomputing every possible aggregation
